# my vision for a community

Day before yesterday, we were having our entrepreneurship cell's meeting regarding our leadership takeover. The feeling you get when taking on that responsibility is difficult, yet exciting. The meeting started in silence, everyone looking at each other's faces, waiting for someone to say something.

I don't know how, but it first shifted to criticising our previous year's plans and progress. While it's quite common to look back and figure out what went wrong, figuring out how you're going to improve is a much harder question.

After that meeting, my biggest takeaway was: **IF YOU TELL PEOPLE WHAT TO DO, THEY WON'T DO IT.** It hit me so hard that I kept thinking about it for over an hour. One option is giving people incentives to do what you ask, great, but in a student club, what incentive can you really offer?

Our club is full of entrepreneurial people who understand the ROI on their time and effort. If you can't give them a compelling incentive, they simply won't do the work. Another idea is strict rules: introduce punishments, levels, or gamification. But will that actually change anything? The answer is no.

After running through it again and again, I landed on one realization: **WE'RE JUST A COMMUNITY.** 

As a student club, if you build a genuine community, most of your operational problems disappear.

To build that kind of community, we need a few things:
* No hierarchy (already satisfied)
* Openness (enough)
* A safe environment for ideas and discussions (already there)
* Freedom (no one is forcing us to do anything)
* Trust (a lot of it, at least with seniors)
* Achievers (crushing things, but not visible enough)
* Knowledge sharing (happening via sessions, but can improve)
* Startup and building culture (needs work)
* Support and networking (available, but underutilized)

You can't force or mandate these things. That never works.

The only real lever is **CULTURE**; intrinsic, mutually agreed-upon values.

How do you build it? The answer is simple: do what you want others to do.

**SHOW, NOT TELL.**

A true community is neither a classroom nor a corporation; it's a shared garage. You don't teach someone how to turn a wrench, but you make sure the garage door is open, the lights are on, and people can see and support each other while working.

For our club's north star, I kept asking: *What is my manifesto for E-Cell?* Without one, how do you give direction to a group of ambitious people? Do we launch projects? Build connections? Host knowledge sessions? Discuss ideas?

Anything you want others to do, do it yourself first. You will only do it if the ROI is real, and that sets the standard for everyone else. But doing it in private isn't enough—you have to put your work in public.

If I want people to send cold emails, I need to sit down, write cold emails, and drop a note: *"Doing outreach right now, feel free to pull up a chair."* When they see the work happening and small wins landing, they follow. The wins turn the group into an open place to build momentum.

If I want people to discuss ideas freely without fear of being copied (a classic first time founder trap), I have to share my own ideas openly first.

Culture doesn't just emerge on its own; it requires someone to set the standard. 

The takeaway: **SHOW, NOT TELL.**

Do what you want others to do.
